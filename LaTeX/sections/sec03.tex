\section{Circle-dependent torsion and its curvature components}

\subsection{Exact derivative-level separation}

For constant $M$, the spatial structure can be diagonalized by a constant spatial orthogonal rotation. Write $\mathrm{d}e^i=n_i e^j\wedge e^k$ cyclically, with constant $n_i$. Round $S^3$, flat $T^3$, and the undeformed Nil coframe correspond to $(4/r,4/r,4/r)$, $(0,0,0)$, and $(0,0,-1/r)$. Let $\dot f=D_3f=L^{-1}\mathrm{d}f/\mathrm{d}y$. Direct use of $\mathrm{d}\omega+\omega\wedge\omega$, including $\mathrm{d}K$, gives

\begin{equation}\tag{D-01}\label{eq:D-01}
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mathrm{EC}}&=W_{\mathrm{LC}},\\
I_2&=6\left(\dot h+\frac23 hV\right)^2,\qquad
I_3=6\left(\dot h-\frac23 hV\right)^2,\\
I_{\mathrm{tf}}&=I_{\mathrm{LC}}+12\dot h^2+\frac{16}{3}h^2V^2,\\
R_{\mathrm{EC}}&=R_{\mathrm{LC}}-6h^2-\frac23V^2+2\dot V.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

These identities hold for arbitrary constant $n_i$ and arbitrary first jets of the two circle profiles. A constant spatial shape changes $R_{\rm LC}$ and $I_{\rm LC}$ while leaving the displayed torsion terms unchanged. Appendix B gives the connection and projected tensor entries. The vector-trace derivative $\dot V$ enters the scalar as a total derivative; the Ricci-tracefree quadratic invariant depends on $\dot h$.

At zero derivative, $I_2=I_3=8h^2V^2/3$, recovering Paper II. In the pure AX circle control, the sum is $12\dot\eta^2/r^2$, with six units from each extra sector and none from irreducible Weyl. The related local first-jet check for a pure axial field gives $I_{\rm tf}-I_{\rm LC}=4\sum_{i=0}^2(D_i h)^2+12(D_3h)^2$. This compares directional coefficients at a point; the actions below vary the torsion only along the circle.

\subsection{Restricted action and Euler equations}

Let $\chi_X=1$ for the Ricci-tracefree model and $\chi_X=0$ for LC-Weyl and irreducible EC-Weyl. After integrating the periodic total derivatives, the exact circle action is

\begin{equation}\tag{D-02}\label{eq:D-02}
\begin{aligned}
S_X=c_M Lr^3\int_0^1\!\mathrm{d}y\,\bigg[
&-\frac{R_{\mathrm{LC}}}{2\kappa^2}-\alpha I_{\mathrm{LC}}
+\frac{3h^2}{\kappa^2}+\frac{V^2}{3\kappa^2}\\
&-\chi_X\alpha\left(12\dot h^2+\frac{16}{3}h^2V^2\right)\bigg].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Thus the LC and irreducible-Weyl comparisons have algebraic torsion equations $h=V=0$ in this family. For the Ricci-tracefree model they become

\begin{equation}\tag{D-03}\label{eq:D-03}
\begin{aligned}
24\alpha\ddot h+\left(\frac{6}{\kappa^2}-\frac{32\alpha}{3}V^2\right)h&=0,\\
\left(\frac{2}{3\kappa^2}-\frac{32\alpha}{3}h^2\right)V&=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The velocity Hessian of the bulk density is $-24\alpha$ in the $h$ direction and zero in the $V$ direction. The highest derivative of $h$ is present for $\alpha\ne0$ even about a nonzero background. The algebraic coefficient of $V$ can vanish; dividing by it would discard branches.

There are two useful consequences within the stated restriction. For $\alpha\le0$, multiplying the first Euler equation by $h$, integrating over the circle, and integrating by parts gives a sum of nonnegative terms:

\begin{equation}\tag{D-04}\label{eq:D-04}
\int_0^1\!\mathrm{d}y\left[
\frac{6h^2}{\kappa^2}-\frac{32\alpha}{3}h^2V^2-24\alpha\dot h^2\right]=0.
\end{equation}

Hence every smooth periodic stationary torsion profile at fixed geometry is $h=V=0$ for $\alpha\le0$. For $\alpha>0$, the family $h=a\cos(2\pi n y)$, $V=0$, with nonzero fixed $a$, yields

\begin{equation}\tag{D-05}\label{eq:D-05}
\frac{S_{\mathrm{tf}}[h,0]-S_{\mathrm{tf}}[0,0]}{c_M Lr^3 a^2}
=\frac{3}{2\kappa^2}-\frac{24\pi^2\alpha n^2}{L^2}.
\end{equation}

It tends to minus infinity as the integer frequency increases. This supplies a derivative-driven unbounded direction already at fixed geometry. Its mechanism is additional to the static bounds of Paper II; it is absent from the torsion part of the other two restricted actions.

\subsection{Local densities and periodic integrals}

\begin{equation}\tag{D-06}\label{eq:D-06}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{T^a\wedge T_a}{\mathrm{vol}}&=-4Vh,\qquad
\frac{e^a\wedge e^b\wedge R_{ab}}{\mathrm{vol}}=-4Vh+6\dot h,\\
\mathcal N&=-6\dot h,\\
\mathcal P&=\frac{\sum_{a,b}R_{ab}\wedge R_{ab}}{\mathrm{vol}}
=D_3\left(2R_{\mathrm{LC}}h-4h^3-\frac43hV^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The curvature--coframe contraction has component expression $\tfrac12\varepsilon^{abcd}R_{abcd}$. The local Nieh--Yan and Pontryagin densities can be nonzero, while both integrals vanish for these smooth periodic profiles with constant geometry. Here $\mathcal P$ uses the exterior-form indices of the curvature two-forms. The internal-dual contraction $\varepsilon^{abcd}R_{abmn}R_{cd}{}^{mn}$ is a distinct scalar. Equation (D-06) identifies the boundary currents explicitly and separates the local curvature contributions from their integrated characteristic densities.

\subsection{Periodic stationary branches and their second variation}

\textbf{Proposition (positive coupling).} On a connected circle of physical
length $L$, all smooth real periodic stationary torsion profiles of the
Ricci-tracefree action at fixed geometry and $\alpha>0$ are the branches below.
Here $z=Ly$, the two signs are independent, and $a^2+b^2>0$ on the harmonic branch.

\begin{equation}\tag{D-07}\label{eq:D-07}
\begin{aligned}
H&=\frac1{4\kappa\sqrt\alpha},\qquad
\alpha_n=\frac{L^2}{16\pi^2\kappa^2 n^2},\quad n\ge1,\\
(h,V)&=(0,0)\quad\text{or}\quad(\pm H,\pm3H),\\
V&=0,\quad h=a\cos(2\pi nz/L)+b\sin(2\pi nz/L),
\quad\alpha=\alpha_n.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\emph{Proof.} On the open set where $V\ne0$, the algebraic equation (D-03)
fixes $h^2=H^2$. Smoothness makes $h$ constant on each connected component,
and the other Euler equation then fixes $|V|=3H$. By continuity this open set
is also closed: a boundary point cannot have $V=0$. Connectedness therefore
makes it the whole circle or the empty set. In the latter case $V\equiv0$
and $\ddot h+h/(4\alpha\kappa^2)=0$ gives the resonance condition in (D-07).
This argument excludes smooth splicing of the algebraic branches without
dividing by the coefficient of $V$.

For perturbations $(\phi,v)$ about $(\bar h,\bar V)$, the bulk second variation is

\begin{equation}\tag{D-08}\label{eq:D-08}
\begin{aligned}
\delta^2\ell={}&\left(\frac6{\kappa^2}-\frac{32\alpha}{3}\bar V^2\right)\phi^2
+\left(\frac2{3\kappa^2}-\frac{32\alpha}{3}\bar h^2\right)v^2\\
&-\frac{128\alpha}{3}\bar h\bar V\phi v-24\alpha\dot\phi^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The zero and harmonic branches have a positive constant axial direction and
negative sufficiently high axial Fourier directions, already at $v=0$.
At resonance the sine and cosine axial directions are also zero directions.
For a mixed constant branch every Fourier block has

\begin{equation}\tag{D-09}\label{eq:D-09}
H_m=\begin{pmatrix}-24\alpha\xi_m^2&\pm4/\kappa^2\\
\pm4/\kappa^2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\xi_m=\frac{2\pi m}{L},\qquad\det H_m=-\frac{16}{\kappa^4}.
\end{equation}

Thus all positive-coupling stationary branches are saddles of this restricted
Euclidean functional. The harmonic branch has zero action difference from
zero torsion; the mixed branch has difference per four-volume
$3/(16\alpha\kappa^4)>0$. For $\alpha\le0$, (D-02) is a sum of nonnegative
torsion terms and zero torsion is its unique minimum. On this fixed-metric
family the LC-Weyl and irreducible EC-Weyl actions have that unique torsion
minimum for every $\alpha$. The mixed constant solutions are algebraic
consequences of Paper II's static term; the periodic completeness and
second-variation statements provide the extension here.
