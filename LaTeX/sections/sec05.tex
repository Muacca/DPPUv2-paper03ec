\section{Torsion-axis orientation and interaction order}

\subsection{The extra nine-component sector sees anisotropy}

Keep the product metric and the $n_i$ constant, set twist to zero, and let $u$ be a constant unit vector in the unrotated four-frame. Respecifying aligned torsion after a constant coframe rotation is equivalent to

\begin{equation}\tag{O-01}\label{eq:O-01}
T^{\mathrm{AX}}_{abc}=2h\varepsilon_{abcd}u_d,\qquad
T^{\mathrm{VT}}_{abc}=\frac{V}{3}(\delta_{ac}u_b-\delta_{ab}u_c),\qquad
u_au_a=1.
\end{equation}

The exact static result is

\begin{equation}\tag{O-02}\label{eq:O-02}
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mathrm{EC}}&=W_{\mathrm{LC}},\qquad
R_{\mathrm{EC}}=R_{\mathrm{LC}}-6h^2-\frac23V^2,\\
I_2&=\frac83h^2V^2+4h^2\sum_{i=0}^2(n_j-n_k)^2u_i^2,\\
I_3&=\frac83h^2V^2,\qquad \mathcal N=\mathcal P=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

In the sum, $(i,j,k)$ is cyclic. Only the extra nine-component sector produces this orientation dependence. It vanishes at round $S^3$ and flat $T^3$, but need not vanish on an anisotropic shape or on Nil. The scalar, LC-Weyl, and irreducible EC-Weyl actions are independent of this constant orientation. Equation (O-02) is an identity for constant orientations $u$.

For the ordered mixing rotation $O=R_{03}(\delta_0)R_{13}(\delta_1)R_{23}(\delta_2)$, with $\tan\vartheta_i=\delta_i$, put $c_i=(1+\delta_i^2)^{-1/2}$ and $s_i=\delta_i c_i$. The torsion axis is $u=O^{\mathsf T}e_3$, with

\begin{equation}\tag{O-03}\label{eq:O-03}
u=(-s_0,-c_0s_1,-c_0c_1s_2,c_0c_1c_2).
\end{equation}

Each $u_i^2$ is even in every $\delta_j$. Thus all pure cubic mixing monomials vanish at $\delta=0$ on this family. The quadratic orientation coefficients in the trace-free model are

\begin{equation}\tag{O-04}\label{eq:O-04}
H^{\delta}_{\mathrm{tf}}
=-8\alpha c_M Lr^3 h^2\,
\mathrm{diag}\big((n_1-n_2)^2,(n_0-n_2)^2,(n_0-n_1)^2\big).
\end{equation}

At the undeformed Nil shape this gives equal $\delta_0,\delta_1$ coefficients and a zero $\delta_2$ coefficient. Expanding around a nonzero $\bar h$ with $h=\bar h+\varphi$ produces the mixed cubic term

\begin{equation}\tag{O-05}\label{eq:O-05}
\left.\Delta U_{\mathrm{tf}}\right|_{\varphi\delta^2,\mathrm{Nil}^3}
=-\frac{8\alpha c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}Lr^3\bar h}{r^2}
\,\varphi(\delta_0^2+\delta_1^2).
\end{equation}

This interaction couples an axial-amplitude fluctuation to two orientation fluctuations. It belongs entirely to the extra pair-antisymmetric curvature sector. Pure orientation cubics vanish by parity, while the background axial amplitude permits this mixed cubic.

\subsection{Field order of the aligned mixed term}

The aligned static term $-16\alpha h^2V^2/3$ from Paper II is fourth order in the field amplitudes at zero torsion. Its expansion provides a comparison with the orientation interaction above. Let $h=\bar h+\varphi$ and $V=\bar V+v$. The degree-two, degree-three, and degree-four pieces of $h^2V^2$ are, respectively,

\begin{equation}\tag{O-06}\label{eq:O-06}
\begin{aligned}
[h^2V^2]_2&=\bar V^2\varphi^2+4\bar h\bar V\varphi v+\bar h^2v^2,\\
[h^2V^2]_3&=2\bar V\varphi^2v+2\bar h\varphi v^2,\\
[h^2V^2]_4&=\varphi^2v^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

At a nonstationary background the constant and linear pieces also enter the expansion. Equation (O-06) expresses the field order of Paper II's static potential in terms of the fluctuations $\varphi,v$, with $\alpha$ held fixed. The cubic terms therefore depend on the chosen background; field order and derivative order label different features of the interaction.

\subsection{Finite-angle extrema and degeneracies}

Equation (O-02) also determines the global orientation extrema at fixed
geometry and amplitudes. On the unit three-sphere,

\begin{equation}\tag{O-07}\label{eq:O-07}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U_{\mathrm{tf}}&=K_u u^{\mathsf T}D u,\qquad K_u=-4\alpha\mathcal V h^2,\\
D&=\mathrm{diag}\big((n_1-n_2)^2,(n_0-n_2)^2,(n_0-n_1)^2,0\big),
\qquad\mathcal V=c_M Lr^3.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

For $K_u\ne0$, Lagrange multipliers give $Du=\lambda u$. Hence every critical
set is the unit sphere in an eigenspace $E_\lambda$ of $D$, with tangent Hessian

\begin{equation}\tag{O-08}\label{eq:O-08}
u\in S(E_\lambda),\qquad
H_{\mathrm{tan}}=\left.2K_u(D-\lambda I)\right|_{u^\perp}.
\end{equation}

For $\alpha<0$ the minimum is $S(\ker D)$ and the maximum is the sphere of
the largest eigenspace; for $\alpha>0$ they exchange. Intermediate eigenspheres
are saddles. The degeneracies are exhaustive: if the three distinct $n_i$
are ordered with consecutive gaps $a,b>0$, the eigenvalues are
$0,a^2,b^2,(a+b)^2$. Unequal gaps give four antipodal pairs; equal gaps give
an intermediate critical circle of saddles. Exactly two equal $n_i$ give
two critical circles, with eigenvalues $0,0,d^2,d^2$. All equal $n_i$,
$\alpha=0$, or $h=0$ make the whole orientation sphere flat.

On Nil, $D=r^{-2}\mathrm{diag}(1,1,0,0)$. For $\alpha<0$ its minimum circle
lies in $\mathrm{span}(e_2,e_3)$ and its maximum circle in
$\mathrm{span}(e_0,e_1)$, each with one tangent zero direction and two equal
transverse eigenvalues. For $\alpha\le0$ the orientation contribution is
nonnegative at every shape and vanishes at the aligned axis $u=e_3$.
Allowing this constant-axis freedom therefore does not lower Paper II's
aligned static infimum over the same shape domain and fixed controls.
This is a corollary of (O-02).
