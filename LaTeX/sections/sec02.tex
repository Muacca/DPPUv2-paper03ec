\section{Geometry, actions, and variation families}

\subsection{Coframe and torsion}

Use an oriented Euclidean orthonormal frame, spatial indices $i,j,k=0,1,2$, and circle index $3$. The circle coordinate has period one and $L$ is its physical circumference. At zero twist and mixing, the coframe is

\begin{equation}\tag{G-01}\label{eq:G-01}
e^i=r M^i{}_j\sigma^j,\qquad e^3=L\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad
\det M=1,\quad y\sim y+1,\quad r,L>0.
\end{equation}

For $S^3$, use $\mathrm{d}\sigma^i=4\sigma^j\wedge\sigma^k$ cyclically; the round physical radius is $r/2$. The torus forms are closed and the three angular periods are $2\pi$. For Nil, take $\sigma^0=\mathrm{d}x$, $\sigma^1=\mathrm{d}w$, $\sigma^2=\mathrm{d}z-x\mathrm{d}w$, with periods $(2\pi,2\pi,4\pi^2)$ and the $x$-face gluing $z\mapsto z+2\pi w$. These identifications are held fixed under variations. Thus

\begin{equation}\tag{G-02}\label{eq:G-02}
\mathrm{Vol}=c_M Lr^3,\qquad
c_{S^3}=\frac{\pi^2}{4},\quad c_{T^3}=(2\pi)^3,\quad
c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}=(2\pi)^4.
\end{equation}

The torsion is geometric torsion, defined by the first Cartan equation, with aligned axial and vector-trace parts

\begin{equation}\tag{G-03}\label{eq:G-03}
\begin{aligned}
T^a&=\mathrm{d}e^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b
=\frac12 T^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c,\qquad h=\frac{\eta}{r},\\
T^{\mathrm{AX}}_{ijk}&=2h\varepsilon_{ijk},\qquad
T^{\mathrm{VT}}_{abc}=\frac{V}{3}
(\delta_{ac}\delta_{b3}-\delta_{ab}\delta_{c3}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

AX means $V=0$, VT means $h=0$, and MX allows both. The supplement accepts geometric torsion directly. The signs and index order of the connection are fixed by

\begin{equation}\tag{G-04}\label{eq:G-04}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{d}e^a&=\frac12 C^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c,\qquad
\omega_{ab}=\Gamma_{abc}e^c,\\
\Gamma^{\mathrm{LC}}_{abc}&=\frac12(C_{abc}+C_{cba}-C_{bac}),\\
K_{abc}&=-\frac12(T_{abc}+T_{bca}-T_{cab}),\qquad
\Gamma=\Gamma^{\mathrm{LC}}+K.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Three quadratic invariants}

Write $W_{\rm EC}$ for the irreducible Weyl tensor and $\widehat R_{\rm EC}$ for the Ricci-tracefree tensor. Appendix A defines their projections without assuming pair exchange or the algebraic torsion-free Bianchi identity. In four Euclidean dimensions,

\begin{equation}\tag{C-01}\label{eq:C-01}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat R_{\mathrm{EC}}&=W_{\mathrm{EC}}+R^{(2)}+R^{(3)},\\
I_{\mathrm{tf}}&=I_{\mathrm W}+I_2+I_3,\qquad
I_{\mathrm W}=\lVert W_{\mathrm{EC}}\rVert^2,\quad
I_a=\lVert R^{(a)}\rVert^2\ (a=2,3).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The component dimensions are $10+9+1$. $R^{(2)}$ is pair-antisymmetric and Ricci-free; $R^{(3)}$ is a four-form. The LC comparison invariant is $I_{\rm LC}=\lVert W_{\rm LC}\rVert^2$. The three actions use $X\in\{\mathrm{LC},\mathrm W,\mathrm{tf}\}$ in

\begin{equation}\tag{C-02}\label{eq:C-02}
\begin{aligned}
S_X&=-\int\mathrm{vol}\left[\frac{R_{\mathrm{EC}}}{2\kappa^2}
+\alpha I_X+\theta\mathcal N\right],\qquad \kappa^2>0,\\
\mathcal N\,\mathrm{vol}&=T^a\wedge T_a-e^a\wedge e^b\wedge R_{ab}
=\mathrm{d}(e^a\wedge T_a).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The minus sign agrees with Paper III's reduced potential convention. No cosmological term or source is added. $\alpha$ and $\theta$ are fixed constants. The static potential $U_X$ is the value of this action on a constant profile.

The static-mixing prescription changes the torsion axis relative to the metric geometry. It is different from simultaneously rotating all fields as a frame gauge transformation. Radius derivatives below hold $\eta,V$ fixed, so $h=\eta/r$ changes with $r$. Derivatives with respect to $L$ are displayed as diagnostics when $L$ was held fixed in finding a radial critical point.
