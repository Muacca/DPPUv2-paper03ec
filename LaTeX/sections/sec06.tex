\section{Coupled periodic shape and twist}
\label{sec:coupled}

\subsection{Curvature components with metric derivatives}

Keep $r,L,\eta,V$ fixed on $S^3\times S^1$, with $h=\eta/r$ and torsion
aligned with the third frame direction. Consider the coframe below and,
for the frequency sequence, Paper III's nondegenerate family (X4):

\begin{equation}\tag{J-01}\label{eq:J-01}
\begin{aligned}
e^0&=rf\sigma^0,\quad e^1=rf^{-1}\sigma^1,\quad e^2=r\sigma^2,
\quad e^3=L(\mathrm{d}y+w\sigma^0),\quad f=e^s,\\
w_n(y)&=A\cos(2\pi ny),\qquad
f_n^2=1-\frac{L^2w_n^2}{2r^2},\qquad
0<A<\frac{\sqrt2r}{L},\quad n\ge1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

First allow arbitrary smooth $f(y)>0,w(y)$ in this coframe. Primes denote
$y$ derivatives, not the physical-circle derivative used in Section 3.
Keeping the first and second coframe derivatives in the Cartan curvature gives

\begin{equation}\tag{J-02}\label{eq:J-02}
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mathrm{EC}}&=W_{\mathrm{LC}},\qquad
R_{\mathrm{EC}}-R_{\mathrm{LC}}=-6h^2-\frac23V^2,\qquad\mathcal N=0,\\
I_2&=\frac83h^2V^2+4h^2\left(\frac{(w')^2}{f^2r^2}+\frac{4(s')^2}{L^2}\right),
\qquad I_3=\frac83h^2V^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

One can obtain the torsion-dependent part without expanding the entire metric
curvature. With $A^a{}_{\mu}$ the coframe matrix relative to
$(\sigma^0,\sigma^1,\sigma^2,\mathrm{d}y)$, let $B=A^{-1}$,
$b_a=B^y{}_a$, and $D=A'B$. The derivative contribution to the structure tensor
is $(C_{\partial})^a{}_{bc}=D^a{}_c b_b-D^a{}_b b_c$, in addition to its static part.
Here $b=(-w/(rf),0,0,1/L)$, $D_{00}=s'$, $D_{11}=-s'$, and
$D_{30}=Lw'/(rf)$ are the nonzero entries. The frame components of contortion
are constant. Substituting these quantities into (G-04) and the curvature
formula (B-02), the torsion difference has zero Weyl projection. Its derivative
part lies in the extra nine-component sector, is orthogonal to the static
mixed part, and has norm $4h^2[(w')^2/(f^2r^2)+4(s')^2/L^2]$.
The four-form part remains constant. Both contractions entering complete NY
equal $-4hV$, giving its pointwise cancellation in (J-02).

The constant $16h^2V^2/3$ in $I_{\mathrm{tf}}-I_{\mathrm{LC}}$ is Paper II's
static result. The derivative term in (J-02) is the additional contribution
from the varying geometry; it is carried entirely by the nine-component sector.

\subsection{Frequency polynomial and strict quartic positivity}

For (J-01), write coefficient functions at the base phase $ny$ modulo one.
Structure and connection have degree one in $n$, so curvature has degree two.
The static coefficients are even under phase reflection, first-derivative
coefficients are odd, and second-derivative coefficients are even. Odd powers
therefore vanish upon integration over a period. Define

\begin{equation}\tag{J-03}\label{eq:J-03}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R(n)&=\mathcal R_0+n\mathcal R_1+n^2\mathcal R_2,\\
\int_0^1 I_{\mathrm{LC}}(n)\,\mathrm{d}y&=Q_0+Q_2n^2+Q_4n^4,
\qquad Q_4=\int_0^1\|\Pi_W\mathcal R_2\|^2\,\mathrm{d}y,\\
D_A&=4h^2\int_0^1\left(\frac{(w_1')^2}{f_1^2r^2}
+\frac{4(s_1')^2}{L^2}\right)\mathrm{d}y.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Here $\Pi_W$ denotes the Weyl projection of Appendix A.
The frequency coefficient $\mathcal R_2$ in (J-03) is distinct from the
irreducible sector $R^{(2)}$. It is independent of the constant contortion and
has LC Riemann symmetries. Consequently all three quadratic invariants share
the same $Q_4$. Moreover, it is strictly positive for every fixed admissible
nonzero $A$. At $y=1/4$, one has $w_1=0,w_1''=0$, $s_1'=0$, and
$s_1''=-L^2v^2/(2r^2)$. The only independent nonzero components of
$\mathcal R_2$ there are
$(\mathcal R_2)_{0303}=(\mathcal R_2)_{1313}=-v^2/(2r^2)$,
which give

\begin{equation}\tag{J-04}\label{eq:J-04}
\left.\|\Pi_W\mathcal R_2\|^2\right|_{y=1/4}
=\frac{v^4}{3r^4}=\frac{(2\pi A)^4}{3r^4}>0,
\qquad v=w_1'(1/4)=-2\pi A.
\end{equation}

Since the integrand defining $Q_4$ is nonnegative and smooth, (J-04) proves
$Q_4>0$, rather than merely checking selected amplitudes. Also $D_A\ge0$,
with strict positivity for $h\ne0$.

Let $\overline U_A$ be the mean static EC action along this path, without
the curvature-squared term. Paper III supplies its scalar derivative coefficient
$C_A$. The exact integrated actions for integer $n\ge1$ are

\begin{equation}\tag{J-05}\label{eq:J-05}
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{LC}}(n)=S_{\mathrm W}(n)
&=\overline U_A-C_A n^2-\alpha\mathcal V(Q_0+Q_2n^2+Q_4n^4),\\
S_{\mathrm{tf}}(n)-S_{\mathrm W}(n)
&=-\alpha\mathcal V\left(\frac{16}{3}h^2V^2+D_A n^2\right),\\
C_A&=\frac{\pi^2L^3}{16\kappa^2r}
\int_0^1\frac{w_1^2(w_1')^2}{f_1^4}\,\mathrm{d}y>0,
\qquad\mathcal V=\frac{\pi^2Lr^3}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

At fixed nonzero amplitude, all three actions have leading term
$-\alpha\mathcal VQ_4n^4$. They tend to $-\infty$ for $\alpha>0$ and to
$+\infty$ for $\alpha<0$. At $\alpha=0$ they return to Paper III's
$\overline U_A-C_A n^2\to-\infty$. Thus negative coupling removes the
high-frequency descent on this particular fixed-amplitude sequence. The
statement concerns integer frequencies at fixed amplitude $A$.

\subsection{A finite-frequency comparison}

For $r=3$, $L=\kappa=\eta=V=1$, and $A=0.5$, the coefficients are
\[
Q_0=0.0003213884274,\quad Q_2=0.01228429824,\quad
Q_4=0.2062093292,\quad D_A=0.2462621564.
\]
The scalar part has $\overline U_A=-44.0019357894$ and
$C_A=0.06430979322$, reproducing Paper III at zero coupling.
At $\alpha=-10^{-4}$ the minimum over positive integer frequencies of this
fixed-amplitude sequence is at $n=5$ in all three models. The LC-Weyl and
irreducible EC-Weyl actions are $-44.7490306345$, compared with the round
reference $-44.4132198049$. The Ricci-tracefree action is $-44.7040679354$,
compared with its own round reference $-44.4092719631$. Positive quartic
growth thus coexists with lower-action configurations at finite frequency.
Both are comparisons of the action along the prescribed family (J-01).
