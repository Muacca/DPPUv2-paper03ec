\section{Introduction}

Quadratic curvature terms in Einstein--Cartan gravity can probe different parts of torsionful curvature. Removing the Ricci traces leaves an irreducible Weyl tensor together with a nine-component pair-antisymmetric sector and a one-component four-form sector. Their distinct dependence on torsion raises a variational question: which components contribute when the torsion amplitude or its orientation changes? We study this question on $S^3\times S^1$, $T^3\times S^1$, and $\mathrm{Nil}^3\times S^1$.

Papers I--III provide the geometric conventions and related variational results \cite{paper1,paper2,paper3}. Paper I classifies radius potentials with geometric torsion and the complete Nieh--Yan term. Paper II establishes the curvature decomposition, the static aligned-torsion identities, and the radius-and-shape potential bounds. Paper III develops the scalar, twist, and shape sectors of the EC action at zero quadratic-curvature coupling. Building on these results, we compare actions with LC-Weyl, irreducible EC-Weyl, and Ricci-tracefree EC quadratic invariants. All three retain the same EC scalar term. The static classification of Paper II supplies the reference limit; the focus here is the derivative and orientation dependence of its curvature components.

The first result is an exact separation for circle-dependent torsion at fixed spatial geometry. With axial amplitude $h=\eta/r$ and vector-trace amplitude $V$, the two additional squared norms are $6(\dot h+2hV/3)^2$ and $6(\dot h-2hV/3)^2$, while the irreducible Weyl tensor equals the LC Weyl tensor. This gives the restricted torsion equations and their periodic-profile consequences. The static analysis uses five independent normalized shape directions and exhibits the nonzero Nil shape gradient and its two exactly flat paths. A further component-specific effect appears under rotations of the torsion axis: on anisotropic geometry, the additional nine-component sector supplies an orientation-dependent term and mixed cubic interactions.

For the Ricci-tracefree action at positive coupling we also classify all
smooth periodic stationary torsion profiles, including the algebraically exceptional mixed branch, and
show that each is a restricted saddle. The constant-axis formula gives global
finite-angle extrema and their degeneracies. A coupled shape--twist family
then extends the component comparison to varying geometry: its additional
nine-component term is quadratic in frequency, whereas a common strictly
positive quartic curvature coefficient controls the high-frequency behavior
of all three actions. On Nil, an integral harmonic-form invariant distinguishes
compact metrics along the potential-flat paths.

These variation families are kept distinct. Circle torsion in Section 3 has
fixed geometry; Sections 4--5 treat separate static variations; Section 6
retains metric derivatives on a specified coupled periodic family at fixed
scalar controls. Section 2 gives their definitions and Section 7 discusses
their scope. The appendices provide projections, derivative calculations,
coordinate maps, reproduction instructions and the revision note, followed
by the fixed-quotient argument.
