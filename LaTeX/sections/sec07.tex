\section{Conclusions and scope of the results}

Paper II's curvature decomposition distinguishes the restricted torsion
variations of the three quadratic-curvature actions. At fixed product geometry,
the nine-component and four-form sectors carry the axial derivative term,
while irreducible EC-Weyl remains equal to LC-Weyl. For the Ricci-tracefree action, nonpositive coupling admits only zero
smooth periodic stationary torsion. At positive coupling
the complete classification consists of zero, resonant harmonic and mixed
constant branches, all saddles of the restricted functional. Local Nieh--Yan
and Pontryagin densities are boundary currents with vanishing periodic integrals.

Constant torsion-axis orientations reduce to eigenspheres of an anisotropy
matrix, giving their finite-angle extrema and every degeneracy. For nonpositive
coupling, releasing this constant-axis freedom does not lower the aligned
static infimum of Paper II. Five normalized shape directions give the Nil
gradient and flat paths in a common chart. The nonzero shape and circumference
derivatives remain, while an integral harmonic-form invariant distinguishes
continuously many compact metrics along those flat paths. Appendix C explains
the gradient term in coordinate transformations of nonstationary Hessians.

On the specified coupled shape--twist family, the extra nine-component sector
alone supplies the additional metric-derivative term. All three actions share
a strictly positive quartic curvature coefficient. Consequently the
fixed-amplitude high-frequency actions grow for negative coupling, descend
without bound for positive coupling, and recover Paper III's quadratic descent
at zero coupling. Finite-frequency configurations can still have lower action
than the round reference when the quartic growth is positive.

These results hold for the specified Euclidean variation families. Within
those domains, the component formulas separate the inherited static
contributions from the torsion-gradient, orientation, and coupled
metric-derivative effects. Extending the analysis to general joint profiles
and to the field and constraint equations of the full theory is left for
future work.
