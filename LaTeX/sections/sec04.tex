\section{Static transverse deformations}

\subsection{Independent normalized shape directions}

Use the Frobenius-orthonormal traceless symmetric basis $\mathrm{tr}(T_A T_B)=\delta_{AB}$ and the single exponential chart $M=\exp(\sum_Aq_AT_A)$:

\begin{equation}\tag{H-01}\label{eq:H-01}
\begin{aligned}
T_1&=\frac{\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-2)}{\sqrt6},\qquad
T_2=\frac{\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0)}{\sqrt2},\\
T_3&=\frac{E_{01}+E_{10}}{\sqrt2},\quad
T_4=\frac{E_{02}+E_{20}}{\sqrt2},\quad
T_5=\frac{E_{12}+E_{21}}{\sqrt2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Here $E_{ij}$ is the matrix unit. At fixed $r,L,\eta,V$, the aligned static torsion contribution is independent of $M$ in all three models. The shape derivatives therefore come from $-R_{\rm LC}/(2\kappa^2)-\alpha I_{\rm LC}$. For an arbitrary constant unimodular $M$, the spatial structure matrix is $N=r^{-1}M N_0M^{\mathsf T}$, where $N_0$ is respectively $4I$, $0$, or $-E_{22}$. This symmetric matrix can be diagonalized to the $n_i$ of Section 3, extending those identities to all five shape directions.

For the round sphere, rotational invariance makes the Hessian a multiple of the identity in this basis. A one-direction calculation with $M=\mathrm{diag}(t,t,t^{-2})$, $t=\exp(q_1/\sqrt6)$, gives $R_{\rm LC}''(0)=-64/r^2$ and $I_{\rm LC}''(0)=4096/r^4$. Consequently,

\begin{equation}\tag{H-02}\label{eq:H-02}
\left.\nabla_qU_X\right|_{S^3,0}=0,\qquad
\left.H^{\mathrm{shape}}_X\right|_{S^3,0}
=\left(\frac{8\pi^2Lr}{\kappa^2}-\frac{1024\pi^2L\alpha}{r}\right)I_5.
\end{equation}

For $\alpha=0$, $r=3$, $L=\kappa=1$, this is $24\pi^2I_5$, as in Paper III. Flat $T^3$ has zero shape gradient and Hessian on the entire constant volume-preserving family, including the aligned MX torsion term, which depends only on the fixed amplitudes and volume. A nonzero torsionful EC curvature on $T^3$ is compatible with these zero shape derivatives.

\subsection{Nil: a nonstationary shape and five path coefficients}

For Nil, $N=-(Me_2)(Me_2)^{\mathsf T}/r$ has rank one. Define $z=(M^{\mathsf T}M)_{22}>0$. Its only nonzero eigenvalue is $-z/r$, so $R_{\rm LC}=-z^2/(2r^2)$ and $I_{\rm LC}=4z^4/(3r^4)$. The full aligned static potential is

\begin{equation}\tag{H-03}\label{eq:H-03}
\begin{aligned}
U_X&=Az^2+Bz^4+U_{\mathrm{tor},X},\\
A&=\frac{c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}Lr}{4\kappa^2},\qquad
B=-\frac{4\alpha c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}L}{3r},\\
U_{\mathrm{tor},X}&=c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}Lr^3
\left[\frac{3\eta^2}{\kappa^2r^2}+\frac{V^2}{3\kappa^2}
-\chi_X\frac{16\alpha\eta^2V^2}{3r^2}\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

For $f(z)=Az^2+Bz^4$, let $f'=f'(1)=2A+4B$ and $f''=f''(1)=2A+12B$. Differentiating $z=(\exp(2\sum q_AT_A))_{22}$ gives the full five-direction result

\begin{equation}\tag{H-04}\label{eq:H-04}
\begin{aligned}
\left.\nabla_qU_X\right|_0
&=\left(-\frac{4f'}{\sqrt6},0,0,0,0\right),\\
\left.H^{\mathrm{shape}}_X\right|_0
&=\mathrm{diag}\left(\frac{32A+128B}{3},0,0,4A+8B,4A+8B\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The off-diagonal entries of this five-dimensional Hessian vanish at the chart origin. At $\alpha=-\beta<0$ and $\eta=V=0$, Paper II's radial slice critical point is $r_*=4\kappa\sqrt{\beta/3}$, where $A=B>0$. Evaluating the derivatives transverse to that radius slice gives

\begin{equation}\tag{H-05}\label{eq:H-05}
\begin{aligned}
\partial_rU_X&=0,\qquad \partial_LU_X=\frac{2A}{L},\qquad
\partial_\eta U_X=\partial_VU_X=0,\\
\nabla_qU_X&=(-4\sqrt6 A,0,0,0,0),\qquad
\nabla_\omega U_X=\nabla_\delta U_X=0,\\
H^{\mathrm{shape}}_X&=A\,\mathrm{diag}\left(\frac{160}{3},0,0,12,12\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The nonzero shape and circumference derivatives make this point stationary only on the radius slice. Its shape second derivatives consist of two zero entries, a twofold equal pair, and one remaining entry in the specified exponential chart. The $T_2$ and $T_3$ exponentials stay entirely in the determinant-one $0$--$1$ block and keep $z=1$ exactly, giving two exactly flat paths. Appendix E uses the integral harmonic-form lattice to distinguish compact metrics on these paths and identifies discrete sign reversals.

\subsection{Static twist coefficients}

In a diagonal spatial frame write $e^3\mapsto e^3+w_i e^i$. Then $C^3{}_{jk}=\sum_iw_i C^i{}_{jk}$, while $C^i{}_{jk}$ is unchanged. On this constant-twist family the tensor identity $W_{\rm EC}=W_{\rm LC}$ and $I_2=I_3=8h^2V^2/3$ hold exactly. Hence the additional sectors have no twist derivatives in this family. For an undeformed spatial coframe, $w_i=L\omega_i/r$. At $\omega=0$ the first derivative vanishes and all three actions have

\begin{equation}\tag{H-06}\label{eq:H-06}
\begin{aligned}
H^{\omega}_{S^3}&=\frac{2\pi^2L^3}{\kappa^2r}I_3,\qquad H^{\omega}_{T^3}=0,\\
H^{\omega}_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}&=\mathrm{diag}\left(0,0,
\frac{c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}L^3}{2\kappa^2r}
-\frac{16\alpha c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}L^3}{3r^3}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The LC-Weyl twist Hessian is zero at round $S^3$; its invariant can start at higher order. Nil selects the noncommutative direction, whereas constant torus twists leave the local curvature invariants unchanged. These coefficients measure static directional costs. For the derivative terms, Paper III finds a vanishing quadratic twist velocity term at zero twist when $\alpha=0$, and Section 6 evaluates a coupled shape--twist family at nonzero coupling.

For completeness, at $q=\omega=\delta=\eta=V=0$ the round-sphere background has $\partial_rU=-12c_{S^3}L/\kappa^2$ and $\partial_LU=-12c_{S^3}r/\kappa^2$. The round-sphere coefficients above are therefore coefficients along the displayed directions at a point with nonzero radius and circumference derivatives. At the torsion-free flat-torus point the displayed restricted potential and its first derivatives vanish.
