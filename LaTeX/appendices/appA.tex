\section{Curvature projections and LC normalization}

For a metric-compatible curvature array, $R_{abcd}=-R_{bacd}=-R_{abdc}$. Define $Q_{bd}=\sum_aR_{abad}$ and $R=\sum_bQ_{bb}$. Do not impose pair exchange or symmetric Ricci. The raw Ricci subtraction is the linear map

\begin{equation}\tag{A-01}\label{eq:A-01}
\begin{aligned}
(\Pi R)_{abcd}={}&R_{abcd}
-\frac12(\delta_{ac}Q_{bd}-\delta_{ad}Q_{bc}
-\delta_{bc}Q_{ad}+\delta_{bd}Q_{ac})\\
&+\frac{R}{6}(\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}-\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Let $S$ denote pair symmetrization and $F$ its four-form part. The three required projections are

\begin{equation}\tag{A-02}\label{eq:A-02}
\begin{aligned}
S_{abcd}&=\tfrac12(R_{abcd}+R_{cdab}),\qquad
F_{abcd}=\tfrac13(S_{abcd}+S_{acdb}+S_{adbc}),\\
W&=\Pi(S-F),\qquad R^{(3)}=F,\qquad
R^{(2)}=\Pi R-W-F.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The trace of $F$ is zero. $S-F$ has the pair symmetry and cyclic identity of an algebraic Riemann tensor, so removing its Ricci part leaves the ten-dimensional Weyl component. In the $6\times6$ matrix of antisymmetric index pairs, the pair-antisymmetric subspace has dimension fifteen; its six-dimensional antisymmetric Ricci part is removed by $\Pi$, leaving nine components. The four-form subspace has dimension one. These mutually orthogonal subspaces account for the twenty-dimensional Ricci-free image. The projectors are self-adjoint and idempotent in the Euclidean sum-over-all-indices inner product; hence their squared norms add as in (C-01). The supplementary calculation also constructs the linear maps on all thirty-six pair-matrix basis elements.

For a product of a homogeneous three-geometry and a flat circle, diagonal structure constants $n_i$ give spatial Ricci eigenvalues

\begin{equation}\tag{A-03}\label{eq:A-03}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_i&=\frac12\big[n_i^2-(n_j-n_k)^2\big],\qquad
R_{\mathrm{LC}}=\sum_i\lambda_i,\\
I_{\mathrm{LC}}&=2\sum_i\lambda_i^2-\frac23R_{\mathrm{LC}}^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The last equality follows by inserting the three-dimensional identity $\lVert\mathrm{Riem}_3\rVert^2=4\lVert\mathrm{Ric}_3\rVert^2-R_3^2$ into the four-dimensional Weyl norm. It gives $R_{\rm LC}=24/r^2$, $I_{\rm LC}=0$ for the round sphere, both zero for the torus, and $R_{\rm LC}=-1/(2r^2)$, $I_{\rm LC}=4/(3r^4)$ for undeformed Nil. These values set the normalization of all tables.
