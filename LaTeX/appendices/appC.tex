\section{Coordinate maps, gradients, and variation paths}

At a nonstationary point the second derivative changes with the second derivative of the coordinate map. If $q=q(x)$, then

\begin{equation}\tag{P-01}\label{eq:P-01}
\frac{\partial^2U}{\partial x^a\partial x^b}
=\frac{\partial q^i}{\partial x^a}H_{ij}\frac{\partial q^j}{\partial x^b}
+\frac{\partial U}{\partial q^i}\frac{\partial^2q^i}{\partial x^a\partial x^b}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Nil coordinate redundancy}

Consider the Nil diagonal parametrization $M=\mathrm{diag}(1,f,f^{-1})$, $f=(1+\varepsilon)^{2/3}(1+s)$. Both parameters change the same diagonal shape. In the normalized basis (H-01),

\begin{equation}\tag{P-02}\label{eq:P-02}
q_1=\frac{\sqrt6}{2}\log f,\qquad q_2=-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\log f,
\qquad q_3=q_4=q_5=0,\qquad
\mathrm{rank}\frac{\partial(q_1,q_2)}{\partial(\varepsilon,s)}=1.
\end{equation}

The corresponding invariant is $z=f^{-2}$. Direct differentiation of $Az^2+Bz^4$, or transformation by (P-01) including the gradient term, gives

\begin{equation}\tag{P-03}\label{eq:P-03}
H_{\varepsilon,s}
=\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{88A+304B}{9}&\frac{32A+128B}{3}\\
\frac{32A+128B}{3}&20A+72B
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

The gradient term allows this matrix to have rank two even though the tangent map has rank one. Its eigenvalues consequently describe this parametrized family; the five independent shape directions are those of (H-01). In particular, the cross coefficient is

\begin{equation}\tag{P-04}\label{eq:P-04}
\left.\partial_\varepsilon\partial_sU_X\right|_0
=\frac{128\pi^4Lr}{3\kappa^2}
-\frac{8192\pi^4L\alpha}{9r}.
\end{equation}

The first term comes from EC scalar curvature and the second from the LC-Weyl invariant, equal here to irreducible EC-Weyl. The additional two Ricci-free sectors are shape independent on this aligned family. Equation (P-04) thus separates the two geometric contributions to the cross coefficient on this path.

\subsection{Sphere and torus path dictionary}

The sphere diagonal chart is $M=\mathrm{diag}(a(1+s),a/(1+s),a^{-2})$, $a=(1+\varepsilon)^{1/3}$. It has

\begin{equation}\tag{P-05}\label{eq:P-05}
q_1=\frac{\sqrt6}{3}\log(1+\varepsilon),\qquad
q_2=\sqrt2\log(1+s).
\end{equation}

At the round point the shape gradient vanishes; if $H_0$ is the coefficient in (H-02), the diagonal-chart entries are $H_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}=2H_0/3$, $H_{ss}=2H_0$, and $H_{\varepsilon s}=0$. Together with the global volume factor, this map gives the normalization in each set of coordinates.

For the torus, compare a volume-changing path with volume factor $(1+\varepsilon)(1+s)$, keeping $r,L,\eta,V$ fixed. With the period-one circle normalization, this path gives

\begin{equation}\tag{P-06}\label{eq:P-06}
\begin{aligned}
\left.\partial_\varepsilon\partial_sU_X^{\mathrm{volume\ path}}\right|_0
&=c_{T^3}Lr^3\left[\frac{3\eta^2}{\kappa^2r^2}+\frac{V^2}{3\kappa^2}
-\chi_X\frac{16\alpha\eta^2V^2}{3r^2}\right],\\
\left.\partial_\varepsilon\partial_sU_X^{\mathrm{volume\ path}}\right|_{V=0,0}
&=\frac{24\pi^3Lr\eta^2}{\kappa^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The corresponding cross term is zero on the volume-preserving exponential shape family. This difference is due to the variation of volume. Although LC curvature vanishes on the torus, the EC scalar $R_{\rm EC}=-6h^2-2V^2/3$ and static trace-free correction $16h^2V^2/3$ can be nonzero and contribute along the volume-changing path.
